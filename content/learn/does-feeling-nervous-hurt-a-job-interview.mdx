On average, somewhat. Candidates who report more anxiety tend to receive lower interview ratings, a link researchers describe as medium-sized for this field. In one study, applicants' interview anxiety was unrelated to how well they later did the job. Preparation has the best support in these studies. More thorough coaching led to higher ratings in an experiment, and in another study coached candidates tended to give better-organized answers. Telling yourself you are excited made people feel more excited, but in a recent replication it did not change how observers rated a speech. Put most of your effort into preparing and practicing answers.

## How much does anxiety cost in an interview?

A meta-analysis combined studies that measured candidates' self-reported interview anxiety and their interview performance. The overall estimated correlation was -0.19: more anxious candidates tended to score lower. The size of the link differed depending on how anxiety was measured and on whether the interview was a mock or a real one. The authors describe -0.19 as a medium effect for this area of research and conclude that anxiety may meaningfully affect hiring decisions in competitive situations by lowering interview performance <Cite k="powell2018interviewanxiety" />.

The studies show a link between how anxious candidates say they feel and how they are rated. They cannot tell us whether interviewers react to visible nerves or whether anxiety disrupts the answers themselves.

## Interview anxiety has several parts

Researchers who study interview anxiety describe it as common. One framework treats it as having three parts: worried thoughts, visible behavior and physical arousal. In that framework, the candidate, the interviewer and the setting all shape how much anxiety a person feels <Cite k="constantin2021tiaf" />.

One questionnaire breaks interview anxiety into five areas: communication, appearance, social, performance and behavioral. It was developed with 212 students and tested with 276 job applicants in a real hiring setting. Together, the five scales had a multiple correlation of 0.34 with interview performance <Cite k="mccarthy2004masi" />. Noticing which area bothers you most, such as finding words under pressure or worrying about how you look, might help you decide what to practice.

## Does anxiety predict how you would do the job?

A study of applicants for residence assistant positions followed them into the job. Interview anxiety had near-zero correlations with job performance as rated by supervisors and by the people the hires supervised. Anxiety did make one rating less useful. For anxious applicants, interview performance did not predict how supervisors later rated them on supporting peers and the team <Cite k="schneider2019anxietyjob" />. The study covered one kind of job.

## What helps: coaching and preparation

A study of 213 candidates applying for promotion into police and fire department jobs looked at who attended a voluntary interview coaching session. Attendance was linked to better performance in the interview, even after accounting for job knowledge, motivation, race and sex. Preparation strategies such as study groups and role-playing were also linked to better performance. Candidates who attended coaching and prepared more tended to organize their answers better, and better organization went with higher ratings <Cite k="maurer2001coaching" />. Because candidates chose whether to attend, this study shows an association rather than proof that coaching caused the gains.

An experiment addressed that problem. Researchers assigned 144 participants to one of three levels of coaching before a structured interview about past experience: an introduction only, an introduction plus awareness training, or both plus skill training. By our calculation, that is an average of 48 participants per level. Those who received more comprehensive coaching received higher interview ratings, and part of the difference was explained by what they learned from it <Cite k="tross2008coaching" />.

## Should you call your nerves excitement?

In a set of studies involving karaoke singing, public speaking and math, people who reframed their nerves as excitement, for example by saying "I am excited" out loud, felt more excited and performed better than people who tried to calm down <Cite k="brooks2014excited" />. In the original public speaking study, saying "I am excited" made people report feeling more excited but not less anxious. A direct replication of that study found nearly identical results for how participants said they felt. It found no difference between the groups in how observers rated their speeches <Cite k="poynter2025excited" />. Neither study tested a job interview, so the evidence that reframing improves performance is mixed.

## Rehearse with a partner

Write down three questions you expect, including at least one that asks about a time you handled a problem. For each, note three points in the order you would say them: the situation, what you did and what happened. Ask a friend to play the interviewer, read the questions aloud and ask one follow-up question after each answer. Afterward, ask which answer was easiest to follow and where they lost the thread. Our [interview answer timer](/tools/interview-answer-timer) and guide to [how long an interview answer should be](/learn/how-long-should-an-interview-answer-be) can help you keep answers to a manageable length. The studies above did not test this exact routine. It combines the role-play and answer organization that the coaching research linked to higher ratings.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it normal to be nervous before a job interview?

Yes. Researchers describe interview anxiety as common. Across studies, more anxious candidates tend to be rated somewhat lower, and one study found anxiety unrelated to later job performance.

### Will an interviewer mark me down for being nervous?

Candidates who report more anxiety tend to score somewhat lower. The studies cannot say whether interviewers react to visible nerves or whether anxiety disrupts the answers. Coaching and well-organized answers are linked to higher ratings.

### Should I try to calm down or tell myself I'm excited?

Saying "I am excited" made people report feeling more excited, though not less anxious. In a replication, observers rated their speeches no better. Practicing your answers has stronger support in these studies.
