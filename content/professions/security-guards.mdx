A visitor who asks a security guard “Which way to the book sale?” may want to buy a book or drop off a box of donations. Before giving directions, ask which one they came to do. Name the destination as the visitor will see it on the sign. A person who has never been in the building can't use “the usual room,” however familiar that name is to the staff.

## Helping someone find the right place

Picture a public library hosting a weekend book sale. The visitor carrying a box says, “They told me to bring these to the sale.” The guard knows that shoppers pay in the meeting room and volunteers accept donations at a table in the lobby. “Are you dropping those books off?” gets to the point. “See the table with the yellow ‘Book Donations’ sign? Priya is collecting them there” gives the visitor someone and something to look for.

Directions are part of the work researchers have seen firsthand. Hyunho Kim and colleagues compared two shopping malls, one in the UK and one in South Korea. They combined observations with interviews. At one mall, officers gave directions so often the observer found it difficult to count the exchanges. Officers also helped customers find lost property <Cite k="kim2018mallservice" />. The two malls differed in how much emphasis they placed on customer service. Their experience gives us examples of what guards say on the job, rather than an estimate of how an average U.S. guard spends the day.

The U.S. occupational data reflect a distinction between face-to-face discussion and speaking to an audience. For security guards, O*NET reports frequency ratings of 4.42 out of 5 for face-to-face discussions and 1.96 for public speaking. Speaking importance is 3.25 out of 5. The occupation's Institute Speaking Demand Score is 52.9 out of 100 <Cite k="onet31" />.

## Explaining a change without making someone feel at fault

At the library, a visitor has an old flyer giving a different location. “You're in the wrong room” leaves them holding a piece of paper that says otherwise. Try “That was the room on the first flyer. Donations are in the lobby today.” Give them a moment to tell you if they'd prefer directions in smaller steps.

In six Finnish shopping centres, youth workers trained guards for a role that included getting to know young visitors. Elsa Saarikkomäki and Pauliina Lampela read the guards’ end-of-shift reports. Guards described ordinary conversations with young people and practical help such as giving directions and finding lost items. The reports tell us what guards chose to record. They don't tell us how every young person experienced the exchange <Cite k="saarikkomaki2022youth" />.

Language training can shape how a greeting sounds, too. During fieldwork in Kolkata, Nandini Gooptu interviewed workers and managers in security companies and training agencies. She documented an emphasis on courteous speech, listening skills and some English phrases for greeting customers <Cite k="gooptu2013interactiveservice" />. If you're practicing a greeting, let your partner give an unexpected answer. Someone who replies “I can't read that sign from here” has given you a reason to name the place aloud.

## Practice a handoff between shifts

Give a partner the old book-sale flyer. Ask them to play a visitor with an errand they can describe in their own words. Give directions, then let them explain where they would go first. If they say “the meeting room” when they mean to make a donation, find the word in your answer that sent them there.

Now switch roles. In one sentence, tell the next guard what visitors with that flyer have been asking. “Saturday’s flyer still sends donors to the meeting room. The organizers are collecting donations at the yellow table in the lobby.” Give the incoming guard a fresh visitor question. Can they answer it without asking the visitor to explain the whole mix-up again?

## Frequently asked questions

### What if I don't know where an event has moved?

Say which part you can confirm. “The talk is still on this afternoon. I'll ask the organizer where they're holding it.” Give the visitor the answer after you've checked. An old room number can sound just as convincing as a current one.

### How do I know if my directions were clear?

In practice, ask the listener to show you their first turn on a public map. If they hesitate at “go left,” try “turn left when you're facing the information desk.” That gives you both the same starting point. For more practice picking up what someone actually asked, try the [follow-up question exercise](/learn/how-to-ask-a-follow-up-question).

### What is the U.S. job outlook for security guards?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects employment growth of 0.9% for security guards from 2025 to 2035. This projection is for security guards as a separate occupation, across all the industries that employ them <Cite k="blsoutlook2035" />.
