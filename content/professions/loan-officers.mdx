Lending decisions rest on numbers, but loan officers spend their days in conversation about those numbers. The O*NET® 31.0 Database rates speaking at 4 out of 5 in importance for loan officers, and the occupation ranks 233 of 910 on our Speaking Demand Score with a score of 69 <Cite k="onet31" />. Loan officers explain credit options to applicants, gather sensitive financial details, negotiate with underwriters, and, for some, call borrowers who have fallen behind. The O*NET category is broad, covering mortgage loan officers, loan underwriters, collection analysts, and loan servicing officers, but nearly all of these roles involve explaining money to people who may be anxious about it.

The occupation employed 274,330 people in May 2025, with a median annual wage of $76,690 <Cite k="blsoews2025" />.

## Where speaking shows up in the job

The O*NET task statements for loan officers include many direct conversations with borrowers <Cite k="onet31" />:

- "Meet with applicants to obtain information for loan applications and to answer questions about the process."
- "Explain to customers the different types of loans and credit options that are available, as well as the terms of those services."
- "Work with clients to identify their financial goals and to find ways of reaching those goals."
- "Contact borrowers with delinquent accounts to obtain payment in full or to negotiate repayment plans."
- "Counsel clients on personal and family financial problems, such as excessive spending or borrowing of funds."
- "Confer with underwriters to resolve mortgage application problems."

Telephone conversations average 5.0 on the O*NET 1 to 5 frequency scale, and face-to-face discussions average 4.89. Dealing with external customers or the public rates 4.55 out of 5 for importance. Public speaking is less common, averaging 2.34, though 9.8% of respondents do it weekly, for example at homebuyer seminars or financial aid sessions.

## What the research says

**A conversation with a person changes repayment behavior.** Laudenbach and Siegel studied delinquent borrowers at a bank where calls were assigned to agents at random. Borrowers who spoke with a bank agent were significantly more likely to resolve their delinquency than borrowers who did not speak with one. Features tied to the human touch of the call, such as how likeable the agent's voice was, significantly affected payment behavior. Borrowers who spoke with an agent were also significantly less likely to become delinquent again <Cite k="laudenbach2025personal" />. The random assignment of agents makes this stronger evidence than most observational studies of how a conversation, and the voice delivering it, affects borrowers.

**The relationship with a specific loan officer carries information.** Drexler and Schoar examined what happened when borrowers' loan officers went on leave for reasons outside the borrowers' control. Those borrowers were less likely to receive new loans from the bank, more likely to apply for credit elsewhere, and more likely to miss payments or default. The costs were smaller when the absence was expected, as with maternity leave, or when departing officers had reasons to pass along what they knew <Cite k="drexler2014relationships" />. Much of what a loan officer knows about a borrower comes from conversations, and that knowledge is lost if it is never handed over.

**Anxiety about sales calls hurts mortgage salespeople's performance.** Verbeke and Bagozzi developed a model of sales call anxiety, the fear of being negatively evaluated and rejected by a customer, along with the urge to avoid specific selling actions. In a sample of 189 mortgage salespeople, they found four components: negative self-evaluations, expected negative evaluations from customers, awareness of physical symptoms such as a shaky voice or blushing, and protective actions such as avoiding eye contact or holding back self-disclosure. These components negatively influenced performance <Cite k="verbeke2000anxiety" />.

## Speaking skills that matter most

**1. Explaining loan terms in plain language.** Explaining "the different types of loans and credit options" requires turning rates, terms, and fees into consequences a borrower can picture, such as the monthly payment and the total cost over time.

**2. Warm, steady collection calls.** The randomized evidence on delinquent borrowers suggests that a human, likeable voice on a collection call helps borrowers resolve their debt. That means a calm pace, a respectful tone, and a focus on a workable repayment plan rather than on blame.

**3. Asking sensitive questions with care.** Gathering application information and counseling clients on spending or borrowing problems involve personal subjects. Explaining why a question is needed, and listening without judgment, keeps applicants open.

**4. Handing over relationships.** The turnover study shows how much borrower knowledge lives with individual officers. Briefing a colleague out loud on each key relationship before a leave or transfer protects both the borrower and the bank.

**5. Managing call anxiety.** The mortgage sales research identified a shaky voice and avoidance behaviors as part of sales call anxiety. Preparing call openings and rehearsing difficult calls gives you a script to fall back on when anxiety rises.

## How to practice

- **Explain one product three ways.** Describe a fixed-rate mortgage to a first-time buyer, to an investor, and to a borrower refinancing. Check the plainest version with the [speakability checker](/tools/speakability-checker).
- **Collection call rehearsal.** Script the first 30 seconds of a delinquency call that opens with respect and a clear purpose. Read it aloud and check your pace with the [speaking rate calculator](/tools/speaking-rate-calculator), since a rushed voice can sound hostile.
- **Leave room for the borrower.** On hard calls, practice asking a question and waiting. The [pause practice trainer](/tools/pause-practice) builds tolerance for the silence while a borrower thinks.
- **Name the anxiety.** If difficult calls feel stressful, the [public speaking anxiety self-check](/tools/public-speaking-anxiety-self-check) can help you see which parts of the fear are strongest, even though it is designed for speaking situations generally.
- **Seminar prep.** For homebuyer or financial aid sessions, time your talk with the [speech length calculator](/tools/speech-length-calculator) and leave half the session for questions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do loan officers need good speaking skills?

Yes. Speaking rates 4 out of 5 in importance in O*NET data, and loan officers rate phone calls at the top of the 1 to 5 frequency scale. The occupation ranks 233 of 910 on our Speaking Demand Score.

### Does it matter whether a collection call is handled by a person?

A randomized study of delinquent borrowers found that those who spoke with a bank agent were more likely to resolve the delinquency and less likely to fall behind again, and that the likeability of the agent's voice affected payment behavior.

### How can loan officers deal with dread before sales or collection calls?

Research on mortgage salespeople describes sales call anxiety and links it to weaker performance. Preparing call openings, rehearsing likely objections, and debriefing after hard calls are practical habits to try. [Real estate agents](/professions/real-estate-agents) face many of the same money conversations from the other side of the deal.
