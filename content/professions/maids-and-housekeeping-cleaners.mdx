Your room list shows 418 as a checkout. A guest answers the door and asks you to come back after lunch. What should the housekeeping office hear? “My list shows 418 as a checkout. A guest answered and asked for service after lunch. I haven’t cleaned the room. Could you check the assignment?” The office can look up the room while you find out which one to do next.

## What do you say when the room list looks wrong?

Give the room number first. Name what the list says, then what you found. If you’ve already been to that room, say what you did there. “I took fresh towels to 321 at ten. Is this another towel request, or is the first one still on my list?” lets a supervisor check two possibilities.

Rebeca Jasso-Aguilar worked alongside housekeepers at a Waikiki hotel. Their assignment sheets showed which rooms were checkouts. During an hour recording the housekeeping office’s side of phone calls, she heard requests from guests and calls from housekeepers reporting discrepancies or asking for something they needed. On the day shift, many encounters with guests went no further than a greeting <Cite k="jasso1999waikiki" />.

In a study involving 75 unionized guest room attendants, one worker described finishing an order and then seeing it appear again on her tablet. She called the office to ask why <Cite k="spektor2023housekeeping" />. You can use the same question whether your supervisor finds an old ticket or a new request from the guest.

## What if you missed part of a guest’s request?

A guest says something about towels and coming back at two. You’ve heard the time, but you’re unsure what they want then. Ask, “Would you like the towels now or when I come back at two?” If a coworker is helping you across languages, tell them exactly which detail you need to check. Before you leave, repeat the arrangement to the guest so they can correct it.

At a hotel in Finnish Lapland, researcher Maiju Strömmer observed one member of a housekeeping team speaking to guests at their doors in Finnish or English. Other team members checked with coworkers that they had understood guests’ answers <Cite k="strommer2021seasonalcleaning" />.

## Practice checking a second assignment

Write “Room 321, towels delivered at 10 a.m.” on a slip. Give it to the person playing the room attendant. The supervisor privately chooses one of two explanations for a second order for 321. Either the first order is still showing, or the guest has since asked for an extra blanket.

The attendant calls about the order. Give the supervisor the room number, the time and what you delivered. Wait for the answer before you say what you’ll do. If it’s the old order, find out who will clear it. If it’s the blanket, confirm which item to take. Switch roles and use the other explanation. At the end, each person should be able to say whether anyone still needs to visit 321 and why.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I report a guest who wants service later?

Pass on the guest’s words as closely as you can. “Room 418 asked me to come back after lunch” gives the office a request to follow up on. If the guest gave a clock time, include it. Ask who can confirm the return visit before promising a time on someone else’s behalf.
