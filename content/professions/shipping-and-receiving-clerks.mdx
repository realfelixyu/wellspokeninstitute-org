An order calls for twelve cartons. Eleven are on the dock. What can the receiving clerk tell the supplier? Start with what you counted, identify the order, then ask for the information you need. O*NET® rates the importance of speaking for shipping, receiving, and inventory clerks at 3.25 out of 5. The Institute's Speaking Demand Score is 51.8 out of 100, a tie spanning positions 642 through 649 among 910 occupations. Speaking matters when two people looking at different records need to work out what happened to the same shipment <Cite k="onet31" />.

## Where speaking shows up in the job

A clerk compares goods with an order, invoice or manifest and records any discrepancy. O*NET also lists contacting carrier representatives about shipping and delivery arrangements, and working with people at the establishment to resolve damage or shortages. Compare the questions “What did you dispatch?” and “What did we receive?” The carrier, supplier and receiving team may each have a piece of the answer. A good first call makes clear which piece is missing <Cite k="onet31" />.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics describes clerks checking whether orders were processed correctly in the company's computer system. A clerk may also use a handheld scanner to track inventory. In the carton example, the order, the scan and a physical count are three things you could ask about. “The order lists twelve cartons. I counted eleven at receiving” gives the supplier a more useful starting point than “Your delivery is wrong.” It leaves open whether the twelfth carton traveled with the others <Cite k="bls2026materialrecording" />.

## What the research says

**Which language works at the counter can depend on who's there.** Dijkstra and colleagues observed and interviewed Polish truck drivers and Dutch logistics staff at two warehouses in the Netherlands. Of the five drivers in their interview table, three preferred to communicate in English and two in German. The six Dutch participants also differed in the languages they knew. A clerk meeting a driver for the first time has a reason to ask what works for that person. The researchers described these workplaces and did not test a check-in script <Cite k="dijkstra2021truckers" />.

**Staff at an outgoing-goods station described the value of another language.** In three Swedish online retail warehouses, Rydström drew on 24 interviews and 15 focus groups with managers, team leaders and workers. At one site, employees described being able to speak another language as helpful when handing goods over to drivers who did not speak Swedish. The wider study examined how workplace language policies affected workers. The example concerns a particular gate handoff and tells us little about what language an individual driver will prefer <Cite k="rydstrom2025warehouse" />.

**A driver may be working against a deadline the receiver can't see.** In 2011, the U.S. Government Accountability Office reported interviews with 302 truck drivers. Of those, 178 said they had experienced excessive waiting at a shipping or receiving facility in the previous two weeks. GAO described the sample as unrepresentative of the industry. Drivers reported that waiting could use up time they otherwise had available to drive. A clerk can tell someone waiting when an update will come, even when the loading time is still unknown <Cite k="gao2011truckwaiting" />.

**Time at a facility tells only part of the story.** A 2018 U.S. Department of Transportation inspector general audit found that the electronic records available to the auditors could not readily separate waiting from time spent actually loading or unloading. An arrival time by itself therefore gives a clerk little basis for explaining what caused a delay. For a status update, distinguish the time someone checked in from the time the receiving team last confirmed progress <Cite k="dotoig2018detention" />.

## Speaking skills that matter most

**Name the unit.** In a fictional call, Rosalind says, “I have eleven on order 604.” Are those eleven pieces, cartons or pallets? Try “I counted eleven sealed cartons for order 604. Can you check how many cartons the packing list says were sent?” If the supplier replies with the number of pieces, ask how many pieces belong in a carton before you compare the totals.

**Let the driver show you the number.** In another invented example, Dmitri has both a pickup number and a purchase order number on a form. Asking for “your number” leaves him to guess which one matters. Say “Which number is labeled ‘pickup’?” Point to the printed label if you can both see it. Read the number back in short groups and give him time to correct a digit. If you cannot establish a shared language, use the help your workplace provides.

**Say what the next update will cover.** Imagine the shipping lead has agreed to call you at 2:15. “I'll ask the lead which door is assigned and update you at 2:15” is a promise you can keep even if the answer at 2:15 is “still waiting for a door.” Giving that same time as a loading appointment would send a different message.

## How to practice

These are invented rehearsal exercises. The studies above did not test whether practicing them improves receiving accuracy or shortens wait times.

- Write a mock packing slip and give a partner a different carton count. Let them play the supplier. Can they identify the order and tell you what to check without asking what “eleven” means?
- Swap roles. Put two similar numbers on a card and ask your partner for just the pickup number. After they answer, repeat what you heard and compare it with the card. Try again with one digit changed.
- Leave a pretend shift-change message. Tell the next clerk what arrived, which count is still in dispute and who agreed to call back. Ask your partner to say what they would do next. If they would call someone who is already checking, revise that part of your message.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do shipping and receiving clerks in the United States spend the day talking?

O*NET's mean frequency rating for face-to-face discussions is 4.86 out of 5, based on 22 respondents. That describes how often discussions happen and does not tell you how long they last <Cite k="onet31" />. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics estimated 816,870 jobs in this occupation in May 2025, so one receiving desk gives only a narrow view of the work <Cite k="blsoews2025" />.

### What should I say if the supplier asks who lost the carton?

Say which count you verified and where you counted it. Ask the supplier to check the dispatch record before assigning a cause.

### How can I give an update if I don't have a loading time?

Tell the driver what you have confirmed so far and who can confirm the next step. If you offer to check back at a particular time, make clear that it is the time for an update.
