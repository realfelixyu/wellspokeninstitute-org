Physician assistants do two kinds of speaking all day: they examine, diagnose, and counsel patients, and they keep a supervising physician and a care team informed. O*NET® rates speaking importance for the occupation at 4 out of 5 and places physician assistants 243rd of 910 occupations on our Speaking Demand Score <Cite k="onet31" />. The team side stands out in the data: communicating with supervisors, peers, or subordinates is rated 4.81 out of 5 in importance. That is the highest rating among the 84 health care practitioner occupations in the data, and only two other occupations of the 910 match or exceed it.

## Where speaking shows up in the job

The O*NET task list for physician assistants has spoken work on both sides of the exam room door. With patients, physician assistants "examine patients to obtain information about their physical condition" and "instruct and counsel patients about prescribed therapeutic regimens, normal growth and development, family planning, emotional problems of daily living, and health maintenance." With the team, they "visit and observe patients on hospital rounds or house calls, updating charts, ordering therapy, and reporting back to physician," and they supervise and coordinate technicians and technical assistants <Cite k="onet31" />.

That counseling task covers an unusually wide range of conversations, from a contraception discussion to a talk about stress at home to a new medication schedule. Each one asks for a different tone, and many happen in the same clinic session.

The work context data match. Face-to-face discussion is rated 5 out of 5, telephone use 4.78, and the importance of dealing with the public 4.32. Public speaking is rated 2.24, and 5.41% of incumbents report doing it daily, a group that likely includes physician assistants who teach or lead patient education sessions.

There is also a conversation that physicians rarely need to have: explaining who you are. Many patients are not sure what a physician assistant is, and that uncertainty shapes the rest of the visit.

## What the research says

**Patient satisfaction with physician assistants is high and similar to physicians.** A 2019 scoping review searched literature from 1968 to 2019 and included 25 articles, 72% of them from the United States. It concluded that satisfaction with physician assistant care was "largely indistinguishable from physicians," with the authors suggesting that when patients' needs are met, satisfaction is high regardless of the type of clinician. Most included studies were descriptive, so the review summarizes patterns rather than testing causes <Cite k="hooker2019pasatisfaction" />.

**Communication experiences drive that satisfaction, and role confusion is a risk.** A qualitative study interviewed 15 patients and patient representatives seen by physician assistants in five acute hospitals in England. Satisfied patients described four communication experiences: trust and confidence in the relationship, sharing relevant information, emotional care and support, and discussing illness management together. Many participants thought the physician assistant was a doctor. The authors saw this as a possible threat to trust and recommended that patients be told about the role <Cite k="taylor2019pacommunication" />. The study was small and set in England, where physician assistants are a recent addition to hospital teams, but the advice to introduce the role clearly applies broadly.

**Patients forget most of what they are told.** A review in the Journal of the Royal Society of Medicine reported that 40% to 80% of medical information provided by health care practitioners is forgotten immediately, and that almost half of the information patients do remember is incorrect. The more information presented, the lower the proportion recalled correctly <Cite k="kessels2003memory" />. For a role built around instructing and counseling, that is the central constraint.

## Speaking skills that matter most

**1. A one-sentence role introduction.** Given how often patients are unsure what a physician assistant does, a plain introduction ("I'm a physician assistant. I'll examine you, and I work with Dr. Lee, who I'll update after we talk") removes confusion before it can undercut trust.

**2. Fewer, firmer instructions.** Given how much patients forget, it makes sense to give the two or three most important instructions, repeat them, and put everything else in writing. When counseling on a therapeutic regimen, say what to do, when, and what to watch for, in that order.

**3. Switching register between topics.** The counseling task ranges from growth and development to emotional problems of daily living. A brisk, factual tone that works for wound care can feel cold in a conversation about anxiety, so slow down and ask more questions when the topic turns personal.

**4. Reporting back to the physician concisely.** The report-back task works best when it leads with what you need from the physician: agreement with a plan, a second look, or a signature. Then give the findings that support it.

**5. Directing technical staff clearly.** When coordinating technicians, state the task, the deadline, and what result should come straight back to you.

## How to practice

- **Write your introduction.** Draft a 15-second introduction that explains your role and your connection to the supervising physician. Use the [elevator pitch builder](/tools/elevator-pitch-builder) to structure it, then trim it to one or two sentences you can say naturally.
- **Cut an instruction set to three points.** Take a common discharge or prescription talk and reduce it to three spoken points. Run the text through the [speakability checker](/tools/speakability-checker) to catch sentences that are hard to follow by ear.
- **Check your pace.** Record yourself giving instructions and use the [speaking rate calculator](/tools/speaking-rate-calculator) to see whether you rush the part of the talk that matters most.
- **Rehearse a report-back.** Practice a 30-second update to a supervising physician that opens with your request. Time it with the [interview answer timer](/tools/interview-answer-timer).
- **Ask for a teach-back in role-play.** Have a colleague play a patient and repeat back your instructions. Note what they drop, and move that item earlier or say it twice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are patients as satisfied with physician assistants as with doctors?

A scoping review of 25 studies found satisfaction with physician assistant care largely indistinguishable from satisfaction with physicians. Most of the studies were descriptive rather than controlled comparisons.

### Should physician assistants explain their role to patients?

Yes. In a qualitative study in English hospitals, many patients assumed the physician assistant was a doctor, and the authors recommended informing patients about the role to protect trust.

### How much of what clinicians say do patients remember?

A widely cited review reported that patients forget 40% to 80% of medical information immediately and that almost half of what they recall is incorrect. Giving fewer points at a time is one practical way to work within that limit.
