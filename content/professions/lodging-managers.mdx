When a guest asks for the manager, find out what the desk has already offered and why it didn't work. A quieter room on another floor might sound generous until you learn that the guest booked this floor to be near a parent. A lodging manager has to hear that detail, find out what the hotel can do, and make sure someone gets back to the guest. Lodging managers score 70 out of 100 on the Wellspoken Institute Speaking Demand Score, higher than 80.8% of the 910 rated occupations <Cite k="onet31" />.

## Taking over a complaint at the desk

Suppose a guest reports a rattle in her room at night. The desk agent has offered to move her upstairs. She has asked twice for a different answer. As the manager, start with what you already know. “Sam told me about the noise and the room upstairs. What should I know before we look at another room?” She explains that her father is staying across the hall. Even another quiet room on the same floor would let her visit him easily.

Now you have a question that housekeeping can answer. “Is another room on that floor ready for a guest tonight?” Until you hear back, you can give the guest a time to expect your call. Save the room number for when someone has confirmed it.

O*NET lists resolving guests’ complaints and coordinating hotel departments among a lodging manager’s tasks. Speaking importance is 4.00 out of 5, and the frequency of telephone conversations is 4.92 out of 5 <Cite k="onet31" />. The second rating measures how often managers use the phone. It doesn't tell you how much of a shift they spend on it.

## What hotels have learned about guest complaints

At a small hotel in Helsinki, researchers interviewed seven reception employees, their immediate manager and the hotel manager. The immediate manager said reception usually resolved complaints. The hotel manager got involved over larger failures or requests for substantial compensation. Sometimes guests specifically asked to speak to the manager <Cite k="schumacher2016recovery" />. That division of responsibility came from one property. A question worth asking at your own desk is “Which decisions can the person on this shift make without waking someone up?”

How you receive a complaint can matter to a guest. In an experiment, 166 people on an online panel read hotel scenarios. When the hotel thanked the guest for bringing the problem to its attention, participants rated the response more favorably and expressed greater willingness to return. The researchers measured people's reactions to a scenario. They didn't track later hotel stays <Cite k="magnini2009thankyou" />. In the noisy-room example, you have something specific to acknowledge. The guest has already spent time at the desk trying to sort this out.

Another study examined 220 complaints recorded in a single New York City luxury hotel's front-desk log in 1994. The researchers distinguished the action the hotel took from the guest's reaction to that action <Cite k="manickas1997complaint" />. Try that distinction at shift change. “Room offered” sounds settled. “Offered upstairs. Guest wants to stay near her father. Housekeeping is checking this floor” tells the incoming manager what still needs doing.

## Rehearse the call back

Put the guest, the duty manager and the incoming manager in different roles. Only the guest knows why the upstairs room won't work. Give the duty manager a minute to hear the complaint and agree on a time for the next call. Then have the two managers change shifts before housekeeping answers.

Give the incoming manager a slip of paper that says no room on the same floor will be ready tonight. Have them make the promised call with only the handoff to go on. Ask the guest afterwards whether they had to explain their reason again and whether they knew what they could choose. Try the handoff a second time with the missing detail. You can compare the two versions using the [guide to asking a follow-up question](/learn/how-to-ask-a-follow-up-question).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a hotel manager expected to give speeches?

Formal public speaking has a frequency rating of 3.67 out of 5 for lodging managers, compared with 4.83 for face-to-face discussions. Both are occupation-wide ratings. A front-office manager handling complaints and a manager briefing a convention group can spend the same morning speaking to very different audiences <Cite k="onet31" />.

### What should I say when I can't authorize what a guest requests?

Name the person who can decide and take responsibility for the next contact. “The rooms director can approve that adjustment. I'll check with her and call you before checkout.” Only promise a time you can meet. If the director hasn't decided by then, the guest is still due the call.

### What is the U.S. job outlook for lodging managers?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects 3.8% employment growth for lodging managers from 2025 to 2035. Its estimate covers the occupation across lodging establishments. An individual hotel's staffing needs can differ <Cite k="blsoutlook2035" />.
