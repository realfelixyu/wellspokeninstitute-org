Few occupations depend on speaking as much as the clergy. O*NET® rates speaking importance for clergy at 4.75 out of 5, with oral expression also at 4.75, and the occupation ranks 6th of 910 on our Speaking Demand Score <Cite k="onet31" />. Clergy speak to crowds and to individuals in crisis, sometimes on the same day: a sermon in the morning, a hospital visit in the afternoon, and a funeral planning conversation with a grieving family in the evening.

## Where speaking shows up in the job

The O*NET task statements for clergy put spoken work first. Clergy "prepare and deliver sermons or other talks," "read from sacred texts, such as the Bible, Torah, or Koran," and "organize and lead regular religious services" <Cite k="onet31" />. They also "counsel individuals or groups concerning their spiritual, emotional, or personal needs," "visit people in homes, hospitals, or prisons to provide them with comfort and support," and "conduct special ceremonies, such as weddings, funerals, or confirmations."

Other spoken tasks include instructing people who seek to join a faith, training lay leaders and youth group leaders, responding to emergencies or crises, referring people to community services, psychologists, or doctors, and taking part in fundraising.

The work context ratings show both public and private talk at high levels. Public speaking is rated 3.78 out of 5, and 61.74% of clergy report speaking publicly every week, which matches a weekly worship schedule. Face-to-face discussion is rated 4.73 and telephone use 4.68.

That range is what makes the clergy's speaking demands distinctive: holding a large room's attention on a fixed schedule, then sitting quietly with one person at a deathbed.

## What the research says

**Sermons vary widely in length by tradition.** The Pew Research Center analyzed 49,719 sermons that 6,431 U.S. churches posted online. The median sermon was 37 minutes long. Catholic homilies were the shortest, at a median of 14 minutes, while mainline Protestant sermons ran 25 minutes, evangelical Protestant sermons 39 minutes, and sermons in historically Black Protestant churches 54 minutes <Cite k="pew2019sermons" />. The sample covers only churches that post sermons online, so it may not represent every congregation, but it shows how differently traditions use preaching time.

**Preaching quality drives congregation choice.** In a 2016 Pew survey, 83% of Americans who had looked for a new place of worship said the quality of preaching played an important role in their choice. Nearly as many said it was important to feel welcomed by clergy and lay leaders <Cite k="pew2016congregation" />. For people choosing a congregation, then, both the sermon and the personal greeting from leaders matter.

**Clergy are a front line for mental health.** Using the nationally representative National Comorbidity Survey of 8,098 respondents, researchers found that one-quarter of people who had ever sought treatment for a mental disorder had done so from a member of the clergy. That share had declined from 31.3% in the 1950s to 23.5% in the early 1990s, but it was still higher than the shares contacting psychiatrists or general medical doctors (16.7% each). Nearly a quarter of those seeking help from clergy in a given year had the most seriously impairing disorders, and most of them were seen only by clergy. The authors called for helping clergy recognize the severity of disorders and collaborate with health professionals <Cite k="wang2003clergy" />. The data are from the early 1990s, but they show how often pastoral conversations double as mental health care.

## Speaking skills that matter most

**1. Structuring a sermon for listening.** A sermon is heard once, in order. A clear main point, a few concrete stories or images, and a return to the main point near the end help listeners follow, whatever the tradition's typical length.

**2. Reading sacred texts aloud well.** Reading from scripture is its own skill: pacing, phrasing, and stress that let listeners follow an unfamiliar or archaic text. Rehearse the reading as carefully as the sermon.

**3. Pastoral listening.** In counseling and hospital visits, much of the work is listening. Short reflections, open questions, and comfortable silence let people say what they need to say.

**4. Knowing when and how to refer.** Given how many people with serious mental health problems turn first to clergy, a calm, specific referral ("I'd like you to talk with someone who specializes in this, and I can help you make the call") is one of the most useful things a member of the clergy can say.

**5. Speaking at weddings and funerals.** Ceremonies call for personal, specific remarks delivered steadily in emotional settings. Gathering stories from family beforehand makes the words fit the person.

## How to practice

- **Time your sermon.** Paste your manuscript into the [speech length calculator](/tools/speech-length-calculator) to estimate delivery time before Sunday, including planned pauses.
- **Write for the ear.** Run your draft through the [speakability checker](/tools/speakability-checker) to find long sentences and dense phrases that work on the page but not aloud.
- **Practice the reading.** Use the [pause practice trainer](/tools/pause-practice) to rehearse scripture readings with deliberate pauses at natural breaks.
- **Rehearse with a teleprompter.** If you preach from a manuscript, the [teleprompter](/tools/teleprompter) can help you practice keeping eye contact while staying on text.
- **Check pace and fillers.** Record a sermon, transcribe a section, and run it through the [filler word counter](/tools/filler-word-counter) and the [speaking rate calculator](/tools/speaking-rate-calculator).
- **Script a referral.** Write two or three sentences you could use to suggest professional help, and practice saying them warmly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long is a typical sermon?

A Pew Research Center analysis of nearly 50,000 sermons posted online found a median length of 37 minutes, ranging from 14 minutes in Catholic churches to 54 minutes in historically Black Protestant churches.

### Does preaching affect whether people join a congregation?

In a Pew survey, 83% of Americans who had looked for a new place of worship said preaching quality was important in their choice, and nearly as many said feeling welcomed by leaders mattered.

### Do people go to clergy for mental health help?

Yes. National survey data found that one-quarter of people who ever sought treatment for a mental disorder did so from a clergy member, more than went to psychiatrists or general physicians.
