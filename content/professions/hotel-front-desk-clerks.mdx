A guest sets a suitcase beside the front desk and asks, “Any chance I can check in now?” When the room is still being cleaned, start with that fact. Then tell the guest what they can do. If your hotel offers luggage storage, “Your room is still being cleaned. Would you like to leave your bag here while you go to lunch?” gives them a choice they can use right away.

## Who do hotel desk clerks speak with?

Hotel, motel and resort desk clerks register guests, assign rooms, confirm reservations and pass along messages. Telephone conversations have a frequency rating of 4.97 out of 5 in the O*NET® 31.0 Database. Face-to-face discussions score 4.58, and public speaking scores 1.92. Speaking importance is 3.75 out of 5. The Institute's Speaking Demand Score for the occupation is 61.2 out of 100 <Cite k="onet31" />.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics estimated 261,420 U.S. jobs for these clerks in May 2025, with a median annual wage of $35,070 <Cite k="blsoews2025" />. Its national employment projections show 1.8% growth from 2025 to 2035 <Cite k="blsoutlook2035" />. The occupation covers clerks at the desk. The [lodging managers guide](/professions/lodging-managers) covers the people who run lodging establishments.

## When the room is still being cleaned

In a study of four hotels in England, Germany and Spain, a researcher filmed nine and a half hours of conversations between guests and receptionists. One receptionist had to tell arriving guests that their room was still unavailable. The receptionist offered to store their luggage, and a guest accepted <Cite k="bengsch2016hoteldesk" />. The exchange settled what to do with the bags while the room was unavailable.

A guest may need something quite different. Suppose they say, “I've got a video call at one.” Ask whether they need a private space for the call. If there's a meeting room you can check, offer to check it. Wait for confirmation before you send them upstairs.

## What does “six o’clock” mean on a taxi request?

In a study at four hotels in Southampton, England, researchers selected 40 recorded conversations for analysis. Some covered more than one task. Of the 46 activities they counted, 12 were miscellaneous requests, including asking for a taxi and leaving luggage <Cite k="blue2003hotelreception" />. A guest can collect a key and ask for a ride in the same conversation. Finishing the check-in still leaves a booking detail to settle.

One way to hear which detail needs checking comes from recordings in four Vietnamese hotels. In a currency exchange, a guest asked for the cost. The staff member asked which currency the guest meant, heard the answer and repeated it. The question narrowed the choice enough for the guest to confirm “euro” <Cite k="vu2018hotelcurrency" />. You can practice that move with a time instead of a currency.

## Practice booking a ride with a deadline

Ask a colleague to play a guest who says, “Could you get us a taxi for six?” Give the guest one detail to keep to themselves until asked. Six is when they need to be at the station. Let the clerk respond. If all the clerk does is repeat “six o’clock,” the guest can easily agree while each person has a different trip in mind.

Try a question that distinguishes the two times. “Do you want the taxi here at six, or do you need to be at the station by six?” Before making the booking, say the request back in terms the guest could correct. “You need to reach the station by six tomorrow morning. I'll check what pickup time the taxi company can offer.”

Have the colleague play the dispatcher and offer a pickup time. Return to the guest and explain exactly what the company confirmed. Ask the guest to say when and where they expect to meet the driver. If the two accounts differ, find the missing detail together. The taxi call is a practice exercise based on the kinds of requests and clarifying questions in the recordings <Cite k="blue2003hotelreception,vu2018hotelcurrency" />.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I say if I don't know when the room will be ready?

Tell the guest what you can find out and how you'll update them. “Housekeeping hasn't given me a time yet. Would you like me to check while you wait?” Once you hear back, distinguish an estimated time from a room that's ready to enter.

### How do I handle a guest who changes a request after I've called?

Work from the last confirmed arrangement. “The driver's booked for five at the main entrance. You'd like the side entrance instead. Let me check that with the dispatcher.” Once the dispatcher agrees, give the guest the new meeting point. If the change is still pending when your shift ends, leave the confirmed booking and the requested change in the desk notes.
