A real estate agent is paid for advice and negotiation, and both are delivered by talking. In the O*NET® 31.0 Database, speaking and oral expression each rate 4 out of 5 in importance for real estate sales agents, and the occupation ranks 251 of 910 on our Speaking Demand Score, with a score of 69 <Cite k="onet31" />. Agents interview clients about what they want, walk them through properties, explain market conditions and financing, present offers, and act as the go-between when buyers and sellers disagree. Clients are usually making the largest purchase or sale of their lives, which raises the emotional temperature of every conversation.

There were 193,370 real estate sales agents in May 2025, with a median annual wage of $52,830 <Cite k="blsoews2025" />.

## Where speaking shows up in the job

The O*NET task list for real estate agents is long, and many of its core items are spoken <Cite k="onet31" />:

- "Present purchase offers to sellers for consideration."
- "Act as an intermediary in negotiations between buyers and sellers, generally representing one or the other."
- "Advise clients on market conditions, prices, mortgages, legal requirements, and related matters."
- "Interview clients to determine what kinds of properties they are seeking."
- "Accompany buyers during visits to and inspections of property, advising them on the suitability and value of the homes they are visiting."
- "Contact previous clients for prospecting of referral business."

The phone is the main channel: telephone conversations average 4.81 on the O*NET 1 to 5 frequency scale, and face-to-face discussions average 4.1. Dealing with the public rates 4.26 out of 5 for importance. Public speaking is uncommon at 1.87, with 4.35% of agents doing it weekly and none daily, so the speaking skills that matter are one-to-one and small-group skills.

## What the research says

**Agents' advice carries weight, and their incentives can shape it.** Levitt and Syverson compared home sales where an agent was hired with sales of homes owned by agents themselves. Controlling for observable features, agent-owned homes sold for 3.7% more and stayed on the market 9.5 days longer. The authors interpret this as evidence that agents, who know more than their clients, have an incentive to encourage clients to sell too cheaply and too quickly, and they found larger distortions where the information gap was greater <Cite k="levitt2008realestate" />. For the speaking side of the job, the lesson is about transparency: the way an agent explains the tradeoff between price and time on market has real financial consequences for the client.

**How a price is stated changes the negotiation.** Cardella and Seiler ran an experiment on how common list price strategies affect housing negotiations, comparing rounded prices, prices just below a round number, and precise prices. A high precise list price produced the highest final sale price, the smallest percentage discount, and the largest share of the surplus for the seller. Prices set just below a round number led to the lowest final prices. The pattern was driven largely by sellers making persistently higher and more precise counteroffers, and the effects weakened as participants gained negotiating experience <Cite k="cardella2016listprice" />. Precise numbers, stated confidently, appear to anchor the conversation.

**Client distress is part of the job, and it takes a toll.** Snyder, Claffey, and Cistulli surveyed 287 real estate agents and brokers about their empathic responses to distressed clients, their communicative responsiveness, their burnout, and their thoughts of quitting. The empathic communication model of burnout, originally developed for human-service workers such as nurses and teachers, adequately explained the relationships among empathy, communication, and burnout for these agents <Cite k="snyder2011realestate" />. Agents, in other words, face some of the same emotional communication demands as people in helping professions.

## Speaking skills that matter most

**1. Explaining price and timing tradeoffs honestly.** Advising sellers on price is where agent incentives and client interests can diverge. Laying out the likely range, the expected time on market at each price, and the reasoning behind the recommendation helps clients make their own decision.

**2. Stating numbers with precision.** Presenting offers and counteroffers is a spoken negotiation. The list price experiment suggests that precise figures, delivered without hedging, carry more anchoring weight than round or apologetic ones.

**3. Interviewing buyers about what they really need.** "Interview clients to determine what kinds of properties they are seeking" means getting past the wish list to the constraints that will decide the purchase, such as commute, budget ceiling, and timing.

**4. Staying calm with distressed clients.** A client going through a divorce, a relocation, or a failed loan approval may be under heavy stress. Responding with acknowledgment and clear next steps, while protecting your own energy, is part of long-term success in the job.

**5. Narrating a showing.** During visits, agents advise on "suitability and value." Pointing out a few relevant features, and staying quiet long enough for buyers to react, works better than a running sales commentary.

## How to practice

- **The pricing conversation.** Script how you would present three price options to a seller, including expected time on market for each. Rehearse it aloud and check it with the [speakability checker](/tools/speakability-checker) so the numbers are easy to follow by ear.
- **Offer presentation drill.** Practice stating a precise offer amount and terms in one breath, then stopping. The [pause practice trainer](/tools/pause-practice) builds the habit of letting the number sit.
- **Buyer interview guide.** Write eight questions that go beyond bedrooms and budget, such as "What would make you walk away from a house you love?" Practice them on a friend.
- **Prospecting calls.** Use the [elevator pitch builder](/tools/elevator-pitch-builder) to prepare a 30-second introduction for calls to property owners and past clients.
- **Check your phone pace.** Agents spend much of the day on the phone. Use the [speaking rate calculator](/tools/speaking-rate-calculator) to make sure you slow down when explaining financing or contract terms.

## Frequently asked questions

### How important is speaking for real estate agents?

Very. Speaking and oral expression both rate 4 out of 5 in importance in O*NET data, and agents rate phone calls at 4.81 out of 5 for frequency. The occupation ranks 251 of 910 on our Speaking Demand Score.

### Does the way an agent states a price matter?

In an experiment by Cardella and Seiler, high precise list prices led to higher final prices than rounded or just-below prices, mainly because sellers then made higher, more precise counteroffers.

### Is real estate an emotionally demanding job?

Research on 287 agents and brokers found that a model of empathy and burnout developed for human-service workers also fit real estate agents, which suggests that dealing with distressed clients is a real source of strain. Agents in related advisory roles, such as loan officers, face similar conversations about money and stress.
