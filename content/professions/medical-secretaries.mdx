Medical secretaries use the phone to find out where a referral went, request a missing document and explain what a caller can do next. For Medical Secretaries and Administrative Assistants, O*NET® rates speaking importance at 4 out of 5 and telephone conversations at 4.90 out of 5 for frequency. The Institute's Speaking Demand Score is 67.2 out of 100, shared by the occupations in positions 336 and 337 of 910. A clear explanation of what the office has received can matter to someone waiting for an appointment <Cite k="onet31" />.

## Where speaking shows up in the job

A fictional caller, Leila, asks when she can come in. One screen shows that a referral arrived yesterday. The document it refers to is missing, and the office has yet to accept the referral for scheduling. The secretary can tell her which part arrived and who is following up. “We received the letter. Our referrals team is asking the sending office for the attachment. There isn't an appointment booked yet.” Promising a date at this point would tell her more than the record supports.

O*NET lists routing messages or documents to the appropriate staff, sending records, scheduling and confirming appointments, and directing telephone calls. Those tasks can occupy different parts of the same conversation. Leila might first need to speak with the person handling her referral and then need to know which number to use if she calls again. When a caller asks what a test result means or how urgently they need care, follow the clinic's procedure for connecting them with the appropriate clinical staff <Cite k="onet31" />.

## What the research says

**A missing attachment can require several conversations.** Knudsen and Bertelsen shadowed medical secretaries over eight days in four departments at three Danish hospitals. In one cardiology department, a secretary described what happened when a referral was missing an existing electrocardiogram, a recording of the heart's electrical activity. Staff might write to the general practitioner, follow up by phone, receive a fax and attach the record before the referral could move on. The report describes that department's workflow. Other clinics may require different records or receive them another way <Cite k="knudsen2021secretaries" />.

A separate case study by Bossen and colleagues followed medical secretaries in two Danish hospital departments during the introduction of an electronic record. The secretaries helped keep information current, complete and correctly coded. The researchers described how closely their work depended on what other staff entered or needed. For a secretary checking a message, that distinction matters. “It's in the system” may establish that an entry exists while leaving open whether the receiving team has what it needs <Cite k="bossen2012medicalrecords" />.

**Some calls end without a usable next step.** Chen and colleagues used trained callers speaking English, Spanish or Mandarin to contact U.S. clinics about appointments for three types of cancer. In 409 of 985 calls across 12 states, the caller received either a date or information about how to schedule. In 32 calls, someone told the caller to contact another clinic without providing a telephone number. Spanish- and Mandarin-speaking callers were less likely than English-speaking callers to reach the study's endpoint. The investigators tested the phone system a patient encountered, including automated messages and transfers. They did not establish that the person answering held a particular job title <Cite k="chen2024clinicaccess" />.

**What an office does after a missed appointment varies.** Richardt and colleagues observed and interviewed medical secretaries in two Danish outpatient surgical departments. Both operated under the same regulatory framework. One adapted its routines to patients' circumstances, while the other followed more standardized procedures. This shaped the follow-up and support available. Long waits and administrative systems available in only one language were among the obstacles the researchers identified. An empty appointment slot alone tells a secretary little about which obstacle, if any, a particular patient encountered <Cite k="richardt2026attendance" />.

## Speaking skills that matter most

**Identify the missing piece.** If a partner clinic asks, “Didn't we send that already?”, say which item arrived and which one appears to be missing. “We have the referral letter dated Friday. The attachment listed on its second page isn't in the referral we received.” That gives the sending office something it can check in its own records. Use your office's authorized channel and its procedures for identifying patients and recipients.

**Explain who takes the next step.** There is a difference between a clinic asking its own referrals team to follow up and asking the patient to call another number. If it's the patient's call to make, identify the receiving office and explain the purpose of the call. Offer to repeat the number. If your office is doing the work, avoid sending the caller away with a task they do not have.

**Check how to reach the caller.** A patient might have given the office one phone number when registering and later asked to be contacted elsewhere. Under section 164.522(b)(1)(i) of the U.S. HIPAA Privacy Rule, a covered health care provider must accommodate reasonable requests to receive communications of protected health information by alternative means or at another location. Check the recorded contact preference and use the office's approved process before you call about the referral <Cite k="hhs2025confidentialcontact" />.

## How to practice

Make up a referral, two fictitious offices and an attachment with no patient information. One person plays the sending office. The other has a folder containing just the referral letter. Have the receiving secretary explain by phone what arrived. When the call ends, each person writes down which document is still missing and who agreed to follow up. If the notes differ, replay only the part where the decision was made.

Then give a third person the part of the caller waiting for an appointment. Explain the status in ordinary language. Ask them what they think they have to do now. If the office was going to chase the attachment but the caller understood that they needed to chase it, revise that sentence. These are untested rehearsal exercises. Use only invented records, names and numbers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does receiving the referral mean the appointment is confirmed?

A clinic may still need another document or a review before scheduling. In the Danish cardiology example, the next stage depended on obtaining the missing recording <Cite k="knudsen2021secretaries" />.

### Can a patient ask a clinic to use another way of reaching them?

Yes. For a U.S. health care provider covered by the HIPAA Privacy Rule, the rule expressly covers reasonable requests for an alternative means of communication or an alternative location <Cite k="hhs2025confidentialcontact" />.

### Which positions do the employment figures cover?

The U.S. figures cover Medical Secretaries and Administrative Assistants. BLS estimated 961,610 jobs and a median annual wage of $45,930 in May 2025 for that occupation <Cite k="blsoews2025" />.
