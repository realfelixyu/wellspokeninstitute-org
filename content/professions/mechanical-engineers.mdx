Mechanical engineers spend most of their time on technical analysis, but the results only matter once someone explains them to the people who decide, build, or buy. O\*NET® data rate speaking 3.38 out of 5 for importance, in the "important" band, and the occupation ranks 433rd of 910 on our Speaking Demand Score <Cite k="onet31" />. Oral expression is rated 4.00, with a required level of 4.75 on a 7-point scale. Research on engineering careers suggests that communication skill is linked to advancement, and that much of an engineer's spoken work happens in meetings such as design reviews.

## Where speaking shows up in the job

The O\*NET task list asks mechanical engineers to "confer with engineers or other personnel to implement operating procedures, resolve system malfunctions, or provide technical information." They investigate equipment failures and recommend remedial actions, recommend design changes, provide technical customer service, and give feedback to design engineers on customer problems. Some estimate costs and submit bids, solicit new business, and supervise production workers and technicians <Cite k="onet31" />.

Public speaking is rated 2.92 out of 5 for frequency: 12.53% of surveyed engineers report it daily and 25% weekly. Face-to-face discussion is rated 4.41 and telephone use 4.43. The mix reflects a job where an engineer may present at a design review in the morning and talk a technician through a repair in the afternoon.

## What the research says

**Graduates put communication in their top tier.** Passow surveyed engineering alumni of a large public university over seven survey rounds from 1999 to 2005, asking them to rate the importance of accreditation competencies in their professional work. Graduates of 11 engineering majors rated a top cluster of competencies (teamwork, communication, data analysis, and problem solving) significantly higher than a bottom cluster. The pattern was stable across years since graduation and graduating classes <Cite k="passow2012abet" />.

**Communication training is linked to career advancement.** Sageev and Romanowski surveyed recent engineering graduates about the role of technical communication in their jobs. Communication skills affected how graduates adjusted to their jobs and reached career goals, and the amount of technical communication instruction they had received correlated directly with career advancement <Cite k="sageev2001graduates" />. A correlation does not prove that the instruction caused the advancement, and the graduates offered recommendations for teaching technical communication in engineering programs.

**Workplace problems come with non-engineering goals and constraints.** Jonassen, Strobel, and Lee studied the problems practicing engineers actually solve. Unlike classroom problems, workplace problems were ill-structured and complex, with conflicting goals, multiple solution methods, success standards and constraints set by non-engineers, distributed knowledge, and collaborative activity systems <Cite k="jonassen2006problems" />. Solving them means talking with people who hold different pieces of knowledge and different definitions of success.

**Design reviews are where decisions get spoken.** Huet, Culley, McMahon, and Fortin studied engineering design review meetings in aerospace through case studies. They describe reviews as a key control point and a unique chance for everyone involved to share information, where key design decisions, experiences, and rationale are often made explicit in discussion. They developed methods to code meeting transcripts and map decisions, actions, rationale, and lessons learned, and used them to show how much of this information is lost from formal meeting records <Cite k="huet2007designreview" />.

These studies are surveys, interviews, and case studies rather than experiments. Together they suggest that for engineers, the ability to explain reasoning out loud is tied to how decisions get made and to career progress.

## Speaking skills that matter most

- **Presenting at design reviews.** Stating the design decision, the alternatives considered, and the rationale makes the review more useful and gets the reasoning on the record.
- **Explaining failures.** A failure investigation briefing should cover what happened, the likely cause, the evidence, and the recommended fix, in that order.
- **Adjusting to the audience.** The same analysis needs a different explanation for a technician, a manager, and a customer.
- **Listening to customers.** Technical customer service and feedback to design teams depend on asking precise questions about how a product failed or fell short.
- **Negotiating tradeoffs.** Workplace problems involve conflicting goals and non-engineering constraints, so engineers have to discuss cost, schedule, and performance with people who weigh them differently.

## How to practice

1. **Write a decision statement.** For your next design review, write one sentence each for the decision, the main alternative, and why you chose one over the other. Say them aloud before the meeting.
2. **Rehearse a two-minute failure briefing.** Take a recent failure or test result and brief it in two minutes using the order above. Time it with the [interview answer timer](/tools/interview-answer-timer).
3. **Explain it three ways.** Describe the same design change to an imagined technician, manager, and customer. Run the customer version through the [speakability checker](/tools/speakability-checker).
4. **Capture rationale out loud.** At the end of each review, summarize the decisions and the reasons for them before people leave, so the record includes the why.
5. **Practice a bid presentation.** If you present bids or proposals, draft the talk and check its length with the [speech length calculator](/tools/speech-length-calculator).

## Frequently asked questions

### How important is speaking for mechanical engineers?

Important. O\*NET rates speaking 3.38 out of 5 for mechanical engineers. The job ranks 433rd of 910 occupations on our Speaking Demand Score, but 25% of surveyed engineers report public speaking weekly.

### Does communication skill help engineers advance?

Survey evidence suggests so. Recent graduates who had received more technical communication instruction reported greater career advancement, and engineering alumni rated communication among their most important competencies.

### What is an engineering design review?

It is a scheduled meeting at a set phase of product development where a design is evaluated. Research describes reviews as moments when key decisions and the reasoning behind them are made explicit.
