Fear of public speaking is common, but the best epidemiological data put it at roughly 1 in 5 adults, not 3 in 4. The National Comorbidity Survey Replication, a nationally representative study of U.S. adults, found that 21.2% had a lifetime fear of public speaking <Cite k="ruscio2008socialfears" />. A smaller share, about 7% in any given year, meet the criteria for social anxiety disorder, a clinical condition <Cite k="nimhsad" />. This page collects 24 statistics on speaking anxiety, communication apprehension, and social anxiety, along with what the two most repeated claims about this fear actually rest on.

## How many people fear public speaking?

- In the National Comorbidity Survey Replication (NCS-R), 21.2% of U.S. adults reported a lifetime fear of public speaking or performing, the most common of 14 social fears the survey asked about <Cite k="ruscio2008socialfears" />.
- The second most common fear in the NCS-R was speaking up in a meeting or class, reported by 19.5% of adults <Cite k="ruscio2008socialfears" />.
- Nearly one-fourth of NCS-R respondents (24.1%) reported at least one lifetime social fear of any kind <Cite k="ruscio2008socialfears" />.
- In a random telephone survey of 499 adults in Winnipeg, one third said they had excessive anxiety when speaking to a large audience <Cite k="stein1996publicspeaking" />.
- In the 2026 Chapman University Survey of American Fears, 28.9% of a national sample said they were afraid or very afraid of public speaking <Cite k="chapmanfears2026" />.
- In a study of 1,135 Brazilian college students, 63.9% reported fear of public speaking, and the fear was more common among women and students who rarely spoke in groups <Cite k="ferreiramarinho2017fear" />.

## What do people with speaking anxiety fear will happen?

- In the Winnipeg survey, the most common worries about public speaking were trembling or showing other signs of anxiety (80%) and having one's mind go blank (74%) <Cite k="stein1996publicspeaking" />.
- Public-speaking fears started early: half began by age 13, three-quarters by 17, and 90% by 20 <Cite k="stein1996publicspeaking" />.
- One in 10 respondents said public-speaking anxiety had markedly interfered with their work, social life, or education, or caused marked distress <Cite k="stein1996publicspeaking" />.

## How common is social anxiety disorder?

- An estimated 7.1% of U.S. adults had social anxiety disorder in the past year, and 12.1% will experience it at some point in their lives <Cite k="nimhsad" />.
- Past-year prevalence was higher for women (8.0%) than for men (6.1%) <Cite k="nimhsad" />.
- Among adults with social anxiety disorder in the past year, 29.9% had serious impairment, 38.8% moderate impairment, and 31.3% mild impairment <Cite k="nimhsad" />.
- An estimated 9.1% of U.S. adolescents aged 13 to 18 have had social anxiety disorder, and 1.3% had severe impairment <Cite k="nimhsad" />.
- Of NCS-R respondents with lifetime social phobia, 88.7% reported a fear of public speaking or performing <Cite k="ruscio2008socialfears" />.
- The original National Comorbidity Survey in the early 1990s estimated lifetime social phobia at 13.3%, with a median age of onset of 16 <Cite k="magee1996phobias" />.
- A survey of 523 Swedish university students found that 16.1% met questionnaire criteria for social phobia, close to the 15.6% reported for the Swedish general population <Cite k="tillfors2007social" />.
- A review of 18 studies concluded that public speaking anxiety is a distinct subtype of social phobia, different in both kind and degree from other subtypes <Cite k="blote2009psa" />.

## How common is communication apprehension?

- James McCroskey, who developed the most widely used measure of communication apprehension, estimated that about 20% of students at major universities have high trait communication apprehension <Cite k="mccroskey1977oral" />.
- On the Personal Report of Communication Apprehension (PRCA-24), norms based on more than 40,000 college students give an average total score of 65.6 out of 120, with scores above 80 classed as high <Cite k="mccroskeyprca24" />.

## Where do the "75%" and "more than death" claims come from?

- The "more than death" claim traces to a 1973 survey of American fears by R. H. Bruskin Associates, reported in the London Sunday Times and later repeated in communication textbooks <Cite k="dwyerdavidson2012" />.
- When 815 college students picked their fears from a list in a 2012 replication, public speaking was the fear selected most often, but when asked to name their single top fear, students chose death most often <Cite k="dwyerdavidson2012" />.
- In the 2026 Chapman survey, slightly more Americans were afraid or very afraid of dying (30.3%) than of public speaking (28.9%) <Cite k="chapmanfears2026" />.

We could not find a survey, study, or government report that measures 75% of people as fearing public speaking. The National Institute of Mental Health's statistics page reports social anxiety disorder rates of 7.1% (past year) and 12.1% (lifetime), and the highest population-level estimate we found in peer-reviewed research is "up to one third" of people <Cite k="nimhsad,ebrahimi2019fops" />.

## Does treatment reduce speaking anxiety?

- A meta-analysis of 30 randomized trials found an average effect of g = 0.74 for psychological treatments for fear of public speaking, rising to g = 1.11 at follow-up <Cite k="ebrahimi2019fops" />.
- A review of research published from 2008 to 2011 found support for treating public-speaking anxiety with virtual reality exposure and Internet-delivered cognitive-behavioral self-help <Cite k="pull2012psa" />.

## How we chose these statistics

We prioritized nationally representative epidemiological surveys, especially the National Comorbidity Survey and its replication, because they use structured diagnostic interviews with random samples. We added smaller studies only where they answer a question the national surveys do not, and we name the sample each time. Fear of public speaking, communication apprehension, and social anxiety disorder are different things: the first is a self-reported fear, the second is a trait measured on a questionnaire, and the third is a clinical diagnosis. Their rates should not be compared as if they measured the same thing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is glossophobia?

Glossophobia is an informal name for an intense fear of public speaking. Researchers usually call it public speaking anxiety and study it as a form of social anxiety; about 21% of U.S. adults report a lifetime fear of public speaking <Cite k="ruscio2008socialfears" />.

### Do people really fear public speaking more than death?

No. The claim traces to a 1973 survey, and when researchers repeated it with 815 students, more students listed public speaking than death as a fear but death was chosen most often as the single top fear <Cite k="dwyerdavidson2012" />. Listing a fear is not the same as ranking it as worse.

### Is fear of public speaking the same as social anxiety disorder?

Not usually. Many people fear public speaking without having social anxiety disorder, but most people who do have the disorder also fear public speaking, and 88.7% of NCS-R respondents with lifetime social phobia reported that fear <Cite k="ruscio2008socialfears" />.
