About 1 in 5 U.S. residents aged 5 and older speaks a language other than English at home, and most of them also speak English very well. The U.S. Census Bureau counted 67.8 million people aged 5 and older who spoke another language at home in 2019, of whom 38% reported speaking English less than very well <Cite k="acs2019language" />. English learners make up about 1 in 10 public school students, and English as a Second Language is now the largest part of federally funded adult education <Cite k="ncesel2024,ccrc2026adultesl" />. The 23 statistics below cover how many people are learning English, how speaking ability relates to work and pay, and what research says about accent.

## How many people in the U.S. speak English less than very well?

- In 2019, 67.8 million people aged 5 and older spoke a language other than English at home, up from 23.1 million in 1980 <Cite k="acs2019language" />.
- About 78% of the U.S. population aged 5 and older spoke only English at home in 2019 <Cite k="acs2019language" />.
- Of people who spoke another language at home, 62% said they spoke English "very well," 19% "well," 13% "not well," and 6% "not at all" <Cite k="acs2019language" />.
- From 2000 to 2019, the share of the population speaking a language other than English at home rose from 20% to 22%, while the share speaking English less than very well fell from 9% to 8% <Cite k="acs2019language" />.
- Among people who spoke another language at home, the share who spoke English very well rose from 57% in 2009 to 62% in 2019 <Cite k="acs2019language" />.
- Spanish was by far the most common non-English language, with 42 million speakers in 2019 <Cite k="acs2019language" />.
- About 4% of U.S. households were "limited English-speaking," meaning no member aged 14 or older spoke only English or spoke English very well <Cite k="acs2019language" />.

## How many students are English learners?

- In fall 2021, 10.6% of U.S. public school students, or 5.3 million, were English learners, up from 9.4% (4.6 million) in fall 2011 <Cite k="ncesel2024" />.
- The share of public school students who were English learners ranged from 0.8% in West Virginia to 20.2% in Texas <Cite k="ncesel2024" />.
- Spanish was the home language of 76.4% of English learners in public schools in fall 2021 <Cite k="ncesel2024" />.

## How many adults are learning English?

- More than 18 million working-age adults in the U.S. do not speak English very well or at all <Cite k="ccrc2026adultesl" />.
- In program year 2023-24, about 1.26 million adults took part in adult education funded under the Adult Education and Family Literacy Act, and 745,936 of them (59%) were enrolled in English as a Second Language <Cite k="ccrc2026adultesl" />.
- Free, federally funded adult ESL programs reach fewer than 4% of the adults who could benefit from them <Cite k="ccrc2026adultesl" />.
- Adults born outside the U.S. make up 34% of U.S. adults with low English literacy skills, compared with 15% of the total adult population <Cite k="ncesadultlit2019" />.
- In the U.S. adult skills survey, 8.2 million adults could not complete the background questionnaire because of a language barrier or a cognitive or physical limitation <Cite k="ncesadultlit2019" />.

## How does English proficiency relate to work and pay?

- Nearly 1 in 10 working-age U.S. adults, 19.2 million people aged 16 to 64, were limited English proficient, and they earned 25% to 40% less than English-proficient workers <Cite k="brookings2014lep" />.
- Among adults who immigrated to the U.S. as children, a study that used age at arrival to isolate the effect of language found that English proficiency significantly raised wages, with much of the effect working through education <Cite k="bleakleychin2004" />.
- In two UK surveys of non-white immigrants, language proficiency raised the chance of employment and a lack of English fluency led to earnings losses, after the authors corrected for measurement error and for who chooses to learn the language <Cite k="dustmannfabbri2003" />.
- Among Hispanic and East Asian immigrant men in the U.S., limited English carried an economic cost, and at every skill level the cost was higher for Hispanic men than for East Asian men <Cite k="kossoudji1988english" />.
- An analysis of foreign-born men in the 1990 U.S. Census found that English fluency and other forms of human capital, such as schooling, reinforce each other in raising earnings <Cite k="chiswickmiller2002earnings" />.

## Does a foreign accent block understanding?

- When native English listeners transcribed speech from Mandarin speakers of English, most utterances were highly intelligible, even though a sizable share of accent ratings fell at the heavily accented end of the scale <Cite k="munroderwing1995" />.
- Among 240 native Italian speakers living in Canada, the age at which they began learning English explained an average of 59% of the variation in how strong listeners judged their accent <Cite k="flege1995accent" />.
- After a 12-week course, English learners taught broad speaking habits such as rhythm and intonation became easier to understand in spontaneous speech, while learners taught only individual sounds did not show that gain <Cite k="derwing1998broad" />.

## How we chose these statistics

We used federal sources for counts: the American Community Survey for language use, the National Center for Education Statistics for students and adult literacy, and federal adult education reporting as summarized by Columbia University's Community College Research Center. "Speaks English less than very well" is the Census Bureau's self-reported measure, not a test score. For earnings, we favored studies that try to separate the effect of English from the many other differences between groups, and we label simple pay gaps as associations.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many Americans do not speak English well?

In 2019, 19% of the 67.8 million people who spoke another language at home reported speaking English "not well" or "not at all" <Cite k="acs2019language" />. A larger group, 38%, reported speaking English less than "very well."

### Does learning English increase earnings?

The evidence says yes on average, though the size of the effect varies by group and occupation <Cite k="bleakleychin2004,kossoudji1988english" />. Part of the gain comes because better English helps people stay in school longer.

### Should English learners try to lose their accent?

Research on intelligibility suggests focusing on being understood rather than sounding native, since a strong accent and low intelligibility often do not go together <Cite k="munroderwing1995" />. Practicing rhythm, stress, and pausing can help; the [pause practice trainer](/tools/pause-practice) is one free way to rehearse.
