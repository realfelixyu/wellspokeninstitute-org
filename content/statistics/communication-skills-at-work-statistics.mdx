Spoken communication is one of the most widely required skills in the U.S. labor market. In federal occupation ratings, speaking ranks above writing in about 4 of every 5 occupations and above mathematics in more than 9 of 10 <Cite k="onet31" />. Employers list verbal communication among the attributes they screen for, and studies in hospitals show that structured spoken handoffs can cut medical errors <Cite k="nace2025outlook,starmer2014ipass" />. The 21 statistics below come from Department of Labor data, peer-reviewed research, and employer surveys. Where a number describes a correlation, we say so.

## How much do U.S. jobs depend on speaking?

- The median occupation in O*NET® 31.0 data has a speaking importance rating of 3.62 on a 1 to 5 scale, between "important" (3) and "very important" (4) <Cite k="onet31" />.
- Averaged across 910 occupations, active listening (3.60) and speaking (3.54) are rated more important than reading comprehension (3.49), writing (3.19), or mathematics (2.55) <Cite k="onet31" />.
- Speaking is rated more important than writing in 77.8% of occupations and at least as important in 90.7% <Cite k="onet31" />.
- Speaking is rated more important than mathematics in 93.8% of occupations <Cite k="onet31" />.
- Weighted by employment, 90.2% of U.S. workers are in occupations that involve face-to-face discussions at least once a week <Cite k="onet31,blsoews2025" />.
- The two largest occupations where speaking is rated very important are general and operations managers, with 3,503,020 workers, and registered nurses, with 3,379,720 <Cite k="onet31,blsoews2025" />.

## Do jobs that require more speaking pay more?

- Across 755 occupations with wage data, the Wellspoken Institute's Speaking Demand Score correlates with the log of median wage at r = 0.588 <Cite k="onet31,blsoews2025" />.
- The typical median wage in the top quarter of occupations by speaking demand is 95,780 USD, compared with 48,745 USD in the bottom quarter <Cite k="onet31,blsoews2025" />.
- Each 10-point rise in speaking demand goes with about 25.7% higher median pay overall, but only about 3.3% among occupations in the same O*NET Job Zone, so most of the gap reflects education and preparation requirements rather than speaking itself <Cite k="onet31,blsoews2025" />.

## Is demand for social and communication skills growing?

- Between 1980 and 2012, jobs requiring high levels of social interaction grew by nearly 12 percentage points as a share of the U.S. labor force <Cite k="deming2017social" />.
- Over the same period, math-intensive jobs that required little social interaction shrank by 3.3 percentage points <Cite k="deming2017social" />.
- In an experiment that randomly assigned people to multiple teams, a measured social skills factor improved team performance about as much as IQ <Cite k="weidmann2021teamplayers" />.

## What do employers say they look for?

- More than two-thirds of the 237 employers in the Job Outlook 2025 survey from the National Association of Colleges and Employers said they look for verbal communication skills on new graduates' resumes <Cite k="nace2025outlook" />.
- NACE reports a gap of about 25% between how proficient new graduates believe they are at communication and how proficient employers judge them to be <Cite k="nacegap2025" />.

## What happens when workplace communication improves?

- After 9 hospitals adopted a program that standardized spoken and written patient handoffs, medical errors fell by 23% across 10,740 admissions <Cite k="starmer2014ipass" />.
- In the same program, preventable adverse events fell by 30%, while oral handoffs took about the same time as before (2.4 and 2.5 minutes per patient) <Cite k="starmer2014ipass" />.
- In a meta-analysis of 106 correlational studies, patients whose physicians communicated poorly had a 19% higher risk of not following treatment than patients whose physicians communicated well <Cite k="zolnierek2009adherence" />.
- Across 21 intervention studies, patients of physicians who received communication training had 1.62 times the odds of following treatment <Cite k="zolnierek2009adherence" />.
- A meta-analysis of 13 trials of communication skills courses for cancer care staff found a moderate effect on observed communication behavior (effect size 0.54) <Cite k="barth2011oncology" />.

## How does speaking show up in hiring?

- In two samples of managers, a combined measure of vocal cues in the job interview, including pitch, speech rate, and pauses, correlated with supervisors' job performance ratings at r = .18 and r = .20 <Cite k="degroot1999vocal" />.
- A meta-analysis of 245 validity coefficients from 86,311 people found that structured interviews predict job performance better than unstructured ones <Cite k="mcdaniel1994interviews" />.

## How we chose these statistics

The occupation and wage figures are the Wellspoken Institute's analysis of the O*NET 31.0 Database and the Bureau of Labor Statistics' May 2025 national wage estimates; the method and downloadable data are on our [Speaking by Occupation](/data/speaking-by-occupation) page. Everything else comes from a peer-reviewed study or a survey publisher's own report that we read directly. We excluded widely shared workplace figures that we could not trace to a primary source. The wage statistics describe associations across occupations, not the effect of improving one person's speaking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is speaking more important than writing at work?

In O*NET ratings, speaking is rated more important than writing in 77.8% of occupations, and the average importance score for speaking (3.54) is higher than for writing (3.19) <Cite k="onet31" />. Both matter in most jobs, but spoken communication is rated higher far more often.

### Do people with better communication skills earn more?

Occupations that demand more speaking pay more on average, but once you compare jobs that require similar education and preparation, the difference is small (about 3.3% per 10 points of speaking demand) <Cite k="onet31,blsoews2025" />. That is a correlation across occupations, not proof that better speaking raises an individual's pay.

### Which jobs depend on speaking the most?

Postsecondary teachers, lawyers, clergy, education administrators, and broadcast announcers are among the highest-rated occupations for speaking demand <Cite k="onet31" />. You can search any occupation with the [speaking demand lookup](/tools/speaking-demand-lookup).
