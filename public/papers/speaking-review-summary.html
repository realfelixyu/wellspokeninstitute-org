Why make time to teach speaking?

A student who can explain a wrong answer gives a teacher something to work with. A nurse handing over a patient needs to know whether a colleague heard the change in medication. Neither task resembles a speech at a lectern. Schools and training programs can give people time to practice these everyday exchanges and hear where a listener lost the thread.

What work asks of speakers

The Institute matched US occupational ratings to employment estimates for 143.0 million jobs. Speaking scored at least 3 on a five-point importance scale in occupations accounting for 85.5% of those jobs. The rating describes typical work in an occupation. It cannot tell an individual how much they speak on a particular shift.

Who gets to practice?

A US household survey asked about situations that had caused fear at some point in a person's life. Speaking up in a meeting or class was nearly as commonly feared as speaking or performing in public. These figures come from interviews conducted in 2001 to 2003. The survey asked about more than ordinary nervousness. Survey questions and estimates.

What instruction changes

English primary schools have tested an oral-language program for children who needed extra support. Trained staff gave children time to listen, talk and tell stories. Trials found gains on language assessments. Gains in reading varied across trials. See the school evaluations.

For an adult language class, a teacher might ask a learner to explain a bus diversion to a partner. The partner traces the route and asks about the first uncertain turn. That gives the learner feedback on the explanation. The review supports practising skills in context. It does not establish an earnings gain from this exercise or determine how many lessons a learner needs.
