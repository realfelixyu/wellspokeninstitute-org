Speaking matters in occupations covering 122 million US jobs

In occupations accounting for 122.3 million of the 143.0 million jobs matched in a new analysis, speaking rates at least “important.” The analysis accompanies a Wellspoken Institute evidence review on where speaking is used and what happens when schools and workplaces teach it.

The Institute combined O*NET occupational ratings with May 2025 employment estimates from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics. It counted each six-digit occupation once when weighting by employment. The 85.5% estimate applies to matched employment. The data describe jobs and cannot measure the speaking ability of the people who hold them.

The paper also looks at when instruction helps. In English primary schools, children receiving targeted oral-language instruction gained more on language assessments than children in comparison groups. In a study of hospital handoffs, medical errors fell after hospitals introduced a package of changes to spoken and written handovers, training and supervision. The hospital study cannot isolate how much of the change came from any one part of the program.

The review recommends giving learners repeated chances to explain, listen and respond, with feedback tied to a task. It distinguishes intervention trials from occupational comparisons, which cannot show what an individual would earn after taking a course.
