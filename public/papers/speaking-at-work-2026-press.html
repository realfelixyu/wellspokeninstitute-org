Speaking rated important in occupations accounting for 122.3 million U.S. jobs

Wellspoken Institute found that speaking rated at least “important” in occupations accounting for 122.3 million of the 143.0 million U.S. jobs it could match across two federal datasets. That works out to 85.5% of the jobs covered.

The rating measures how much an occupation depends on conveying information by talking. It includes a nurse explaining discharge instructions to a patient and a manager assigning work at the start of a shift. Registered nurses and general and operations managers both scored 4 out of 5, or “very important.” Federal employment estimates put those occupations at about 3.38 million and 3.50 million jobs, respectively.

For the research brief Speaking at Work 2026, published November 24, 2026, the Institute used speaking ratings for 910 occupations from the O*NET® 31.0 Database. It matched the ratings to 759 federal occupational categories with employment estimates in the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics’ May 2025 Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics. Each category’s employment counted once. When several specialties shared a category, the Institute used the main occupation’s rating where available and averaged the specialty ratings otherwise.

At the higher threshold of “very important,” the matched occupations accounted for 37.8 million jobs. These estimates describe the demands of occupations. Individual workers in the same occupation can spend different amounts of time speaking.
